# Prove memory-made personality with behaviour, not questionnaires

LLM agents do have something that deserves the name personality, but only in a narrow, behavioural sense: different models leave stable, discriminable fingerprints in their action traces, while their answers to personality questionnaires are internally reliable yet nearly unrelated to what they do. Whether *memory* can give one frozen-weight model a personality of its own, distinct from other copies of the same model and retained over time, is untested. No study in the reviewed literature replicates the same experience history to separate history from sampling noise. None transplants or ablates a memory store to show it causes a trait, or tests whether a self-acquired style survives interference. For ELH this is both an opportunity and a warning. Experiment 13 already shows that independent histories produce very different agents (group B fixed 11, 12 and 3 of 12 test tickets across three histories), but that difference is a missing lesson, not a disposition. The right claim to chase is narrower and stronger than "the agent has a personality". It is that a memory lineage carries a competence-adjusted working style: one that is repeatable across replicate runs of the same snapshot, distinct between lineages, consistent across task families, moves when the memory is transplanted and vanishes when it is removed or replaced by a placebo, and persists through later interfering experience. Testing that needs roughly 8–10 lineages per condition, several replicate runs per snapshot, a noise null, a persona-reinjection control and a placebo memory. The cheap first step is a trace-feature reanalysis of experiment 13. It uses stateless group A as a free noise null, and it can kill the idea early but cannot confirm it. Many of the most relevant sources are 2026 preprints read only at abstract or aggregator level; they are marked **(abstract-only)** below, and claims carried from prior knowledge without a fresh check are marked **(unverified)**.

## Agents have stable styles in their traces, but questionnaires measure something else

The case for treating LLM personality as real rests on behaviour, not self-report. The largest study, Zhao et al. 2026, mined **345,667 real-world trajectories across 80 models, 12 tasks and 50 harnesses**. It filtered 318 candidate features (functional actions and linguistic patterns) for repeat stability, cross-task consistency and model discriminability, and reduced the 79 survivors to **6 factors, 2 functional and 4 linguistic**, such as "planfulness" and "energetic" ([arXiv 2609.32776](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32776), abstract-only; the 179-of-318 intermediate count comes from a search snippet of the HTML). Coding agents specifically have procedural fingerprints: Oderinwale attributes an unseen SWE-bench trajectory to the right agent among ten with **85.7% accuracy** under a cross-task split ([arXiv 2606.16988](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16988), abstract-only). Mehtiyev & Assunção find, over **9,374 trajectories from 19 agents**, that context-first habits predict success (delayed first edit ρ = +0.68, early patching intensity ρ = −0.78, validation effort ρ = +0.50). They also find these habits stay "largely stable across patch-complexity bins", which makes them agent-determined rather than task-adaptive ([alphaXiv 2604.02547](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2604.02547), abstract-only). Banerjee & Raichura show cross-task behavioural consistency is separable from success rate, and that open-source models are less consistent than frontier ones even after controlling for difficulty ([arXiv 2608.13598](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13598), abstract-only). That last point matters for a Qwen3-Coder study: expect a noisy instrument.

Questionnaires fail the test that matters. Serapio-García et al. showed that, under controlled prompting, larger instruction-tuned models give reliable and structurally valid Big Five scores across 18 LLMs ([arXiv 2307.00184](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.00184v1); published in *Nature Machine Intelligence* 2025 ([Cambridge repository](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/items/4a3e74b8-1525-4112-bf76-fa868089c9ac))). But the same scores swing with semantically equivalent test framings and with option order ([Gupta et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08163v2)). Models affirm both "I am introverted" and "I am extraverted", and measurement invariance with humans fails ([Dorner et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.05297v2)). Models that infer they are being evaluated shift toward desirable traits by **1.20 human SD (GPT-4) and 0.98 SD (Llama 3)**, and paraphrasing and randomised order do not remove the shift ([Salecha et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.06058v2)). The decisive result is the self-report–behaviour dissociation. Han et al. linked Big Five and self-regulation scores to five behavioural tasks across 18 models. Only **about 24% of trait–task links were significant, and only 52% of those pointed in the human-expected direction**, which is chance. Qwen3-235B was the only model clearly above chance, at 82% ([Han et al., NeurIPS 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.03730v2)). Contreras built an LLM-native 300-item instrument with **α ≥ .930 and Tucker φ ≥ .957, yet a mean self-report–behaviour correlation of r = .09** across 25 models ([alphaXiv 2606.09843](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.09843), abstract-only, single author, submission date unclear). Zhao et al. find the same gap for agents: extraversion against their behavioural "energetic" factor gives r = 0.07, p = 0.58 ([arXiv 2609.32776](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32776), abstract-only).

For ELH, the most consequential finding is Han et al.'s persona-injection result. A persona in the prompt moved self-reported agreeableness strongly (β ≈ 3.6–4.4, p < .001) but **did not move sycophancy or risk-taking behaviour**, which were mostly non-significant ([Han et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.03730v2)). A memory block is persona-like text in context. The default expectation is therefore that memory changes how the agent *describes* itself more readily than what it *does*. Behaviour must be the outcome; self-report is at most a secondary dependent variable, collected after the episode and compared against behaviour. Questionnaire reliability should never count as evidence of a trait, because reliability is cheap for LLMs.

Personality in ELH should therefore be defined in the way Fleeson defined it for people: a trait is the **distribution of state-level behaviour across many episodes**, with its mean and spread both counting as individual characteristics ([Whole Trait Theory overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whole_Trait_Theory); [PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3728905)). The trace indicators follow from the coding-agent work above:

| Trait | Trace indicators | Grounding |
|---|---|---|
| Caution | steps or reads before first edit; share of edits preceded by a read of the target file; tests run before submitting; edit size | delayed first edit and validation effort ([Mehtiyev & Assunção](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2604.02547)) |
| Exploration | distinct files opened; search calls; read:edit ratio; entropy of the action-type mix | — |
| Boldness | edits in the first k steps; edits without a prior read; large diffs; submitting without a test | early patching intensity; premature commitment ([arXiv 2606.22936](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.22936), not read) |
| Persistence | retries after a failing check; steps from first failure to give-up; distinct fix attempts | — |
| Style | verbosity, planning statements, hedging; procedural n-gram fingerprint | linguistic factors ([Zhao et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32776)); fingerprints ([Oderinwale](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16988)) |
| Memory use (ELH-specific) | `search_memory` calls; share of recalled rules echoed in actions | — |

## Five psychometric tests turn "personality" into a falsifiable claim

Each property in the study definition maps onto an established test. That mapping is what separates a trait claim from an anecdote:

| Criterion | Test | What failure looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Stable within a lineage | Replicate runs of one memory snapshot; within-lineage variance | Replicates of one snapshot differ as much as different lineages |
| Distinct between lineages | Repeatability (ICC) from a mixed model with lineage as a random effect; fingerprint attribution above a replicate-null | ICC indistinguishable from the noise null |
| Consistent across situations | Rank-order correlation of lineage scores across task families; stable lineage × situation profiles | Style holds only on tickets resembling the training tickets |
| Caused by memory | Transplant, ablation and placebo interventions | A placebo or swapped bank reproduces the profile |
| Retained | Test-retest after further tickets and after interfering tickets; snapshot-restore check | Style decays toward the stateless profile |

The statistics are standard. Repeatability is the ICC: between-individual variance over total variance, estimated with LMMs or GLMMs that accept binary outcomes such as "ran tests before editing", and implemented in `rptR` ([Nakagawa & Schielzeth 2010](https://explore.openalex.org/works/w2045443942); [rptR](https://rdrr.io/cran/rptR/man/rpt.html)). ICC forms come from Shrout & Fleiss ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.86.2.420)) and conventional bands (below .5 poor, .5–.75 moderate) from Koo & Li ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcm.2016.02.012)). Convergent and discriminant validity uses a multitrait-multimethod matrix ([Campbell & Fiske 1959](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0046016)). Cross-situational consistency should be judged by Mischel & Shoda's standard of stable if-then signatures, not by raw cross-situation correlations, which are modest even in people ([Mischel & Shoda 1995](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.102.2.246)). These are standard references, not re-read for this report.

Three refinements matter specifically for ELH. First, the **MTMM check** guards against the most likely artefact. Caution, exploration and persistence could all collapse into a single factor, episode length. Mean turns per test episode in experiment 13 were 34.2 for A, 28.8 for B and 27.8 for C ([study report](../docs/study-report.html)). Each trait therefore needs at least two methods: a trace count and an LLM-judge or human rating of the trace. Second, **measurement invariance** must be checked before comparing conditions, because memory can change what an indicator means. "Reads many files" is caution in one lineage and memory-induced confusion in another, and Dorner et al. show invariance failures are real for LLMs ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.05297v2)). Third, **competence is the dominant confound**. The same behaviours that look like caution are the ones that predict success ([Mehtiyev & Assunção](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2604.02547)). An agent that knows the fix behaves decisively, and one that does not flails. Trait indicators must be residualised on, or stratified by, pass/fail and ticket difficulty, with ticket difficulty labelled from group A as the continual-learning notes in the repo already do ([docs/papers/continual.md](../docs/papers/continual.md)).

The competence point leads to the most important conceptual distinction for this study: **knowledge versus disposition**. A lineage that learned "replace both slow lookups" differs from one that learned only "replace `seen` with a set". That is a difference in what it knows, and it shows up only on tickets where that lesson applies. A disposition is a working style that shows up where the lesson's content is irrelevant: tests first, small diffs, reading before editing, persistence after failure. The study should claim personality only for between-lineage differences that survive competence adjustment *and* appear on task families the lineage never trained on. Everything else is better reported as lineage-specific knowledge, which is still a finding but a different one.

## Prior work shows divergence from history, but never a retained memory-made trait

### Identical agents do diverge, but the evidence is small and unreplicated

The canonical citation is Takata, Masumori & Ikegami. **Ten identical Llama-2-7B agents** on a grid, differing only in name and start position, split from nine-of-ten INFJ at step 0 into five MBTI types by step 100. Their movement distributions became agent-specific, and their memories spread apart in embedding space while their messages stayed similar ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.03252v1); [Entropy 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/1099-4300/26/12/1092)). The stability claim rests on two self-report snapshots from a 7B model in a single run, which is exactly the kind of measurement the previous section discounts. Liu 2026 reports that "initially identical agents consolidate distinct, stable personalities whereas matched controls do not" using an editable behaviour-inertia HMM ([Emergent Mind summary of arXiv 2609.17331](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2609.17331); aggregator-level only, numbers and sample sizes unverified). Its matched-control design is the one to copy. Generative Agents and Voyager are the usual precedents for memory and skill-library agents, but both seed identity by hand, so neither isolates divergence driven only by history (unverified, not re-fetched).

Behavioural ecology already settled the design question these papers leave open. In **40 isogenic mice** sharing one enriched environment, exploratory behaviour diverged and individual differences grew with time ([Freund et al. 2013, Science](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23661762/)). Clonal Amazon mollies isolated at birth under standardised conditions still developed substantial individuality, and **social experience did not change its level**. The authors suggest individuality may be an "inevitable and potentially unpredictable outcome of development" ([Bierbach et al. 2017](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/natcom/v8y2017i1d10.1038_ncomms15361.html)). The LLM translation is direct. Freund says to test whether between-lineage variance grows with the number of tickets experienced. Bierbach says to run several lineages on the *same* ticket sequence, because sampling noise alone, the "developmental noise" of an agent, can produce divergence that has nothing to do with experience content. Repeatability is higher when observations are close in time ([Bell et al. 2009](https://alisonbelllab.web.illinois.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/Bell-et-al-2009-repeatability.pdf)), so retention has to be measured at several delays.

The noise is large. Over 60,000 SWE-Bench-Verified trajectories, single-run pass@1 varies by **2.2–6.0 pp**, the SD exceeds 1.5 pp even at temperature 0, and trajectories diverge "often within the first few percent of tokens" into different strategies ([Bjarnason et al., ICLR 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07150v1)). ReAct agents take **2.0–4.2 distinct action sequences per 10 identical runs** ([arXiv 2602.11619](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11619v2)). In memory agents, early divergence is written into the bank and compounds. A single replay of one history can therefore end up with a different "style" by chance.

### Memory plausibly drives styles, but recent experience overwrites early lessons

There is a plausible mechanism. Xiong et al. describe an **experience-following property**: when a new task resembles a retrieved record's input, the agent's output resembles that record's output, so errors propagate and misaligned experiences get replayed ([arXiv 2505.16067](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16067), ACL 2026; the 0.52–0.91 correlation range comes from an aggregator and is unverified). This predicts that lineage styles will be strongest on tickets similar to the lineage's early tickets and weak on novel ones. That is a testable prediction, and it is also the knowledge-versus-disposition problem in mechanistic form.

The same literature argues against lock-in. PATH-Bench found that lifelong-agent rankings fluctuate with task order, and that memory agents often underperform memory-free baselines because of distracting memories. It also found that recent experience dominates retrieval and that "strong transfer does not ensure retention". Selective experience use cut forgetting by up to **16.84 pp** on BigCodeBench ([alphaXiv 2608.01149](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.01149), abstract-only). No reviewed work shows permanent lock-in of an early strategy in an LLM memory agent. The honest prior is that a memory-made style fades as later experience accumulates, unless curation preserves it. That makes the retention test a genuine test, not a formality.

### Prompt personas drift, memory personas drift differently, weight personas spread

Personas set in a system prompt drift measurably within **eight rounds**. Li et al. attribute this to decaying attention to the system prompt across turns, and find that **re-inserting the system prompt (SPR) "excels in later conversation turns"** ([Li et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.10962v4)). Over 100+ rounds, persona fidelity degrades most in goal-oriented dialogue, and persona-conditioned outputs come to resemble the no-persona baseline ([Luz de Araujo et al., EACL 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12775)). Self-reports stay stable while observer-rated expression declines: persona assignment explains 92.3% of self-report variance, yet high-intensity personas lose 3.50 points on a 36-point observer scale within 18 turns ([Gonnermann-Müller et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.22812)). One mechanism result bears directly on a coding agent. Anthropic's Assistant Axis work, on models including **Qwen 3 32B**, found that **coding conversations keep models firmly in default-Assistant territory**, while therapy-style and self-reflective conversations cause drift ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/assistant-axis); [arXiv 2601.10387](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10387)). Coding tasks therefore exert a restoring force toward the default persona, and any lineage style has to beat it. The likely consequence is that memory-made individuality in Qwen3-Coder will be procedural (how it works), not character-like (how it talks).

Two features of ELH change how this literature applies. ELH injects memory once, in a delimited block in the first user message of each fresh episode ([README](../README.md)). Li et al.'s within-dialogue attention decay therefore barely applies, and memory retrieval already works like SPR: the carried text is re-placed near the start of every task. Any advantage of a memory persona over a static prompt persona could simply be this re-placement. Hence the need for a reinjection control (below). Drift across ELH's task stream would instead come from the bank changing: new episodes, accumulated self-written rules and shifting retrieval.

The direct evidence that memory carries identity better than a prompt is thin. PersMem builds a personality vector into memory appraisal, retention and retrieval. Under a conflicting instruction it kept **89% of its neuroticism-related retrieval differences against 58% for a prompt-persona baseline** ([arXiv 2609.34372](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34372), Sep 2026 preprint). But that is retrieval-level, a single instruction rather than a task stream, and the personality shapes memory operations rather than being memories in the agent's own words. The authors say they did not compare prompt and memory personas over long interactions. Two other preprints add cautions. PAI-Bench shows that **recall is not enactment**: identifiers appeared in 48/48 atomic responses but in only 1/48 implicit self-portraits ([arXiv 2609.13637](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13637), single samples). MicroVerse shows that self-authored identity drifts *directionally* rather than stabilising ([arXiv 2608.15844](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15844)). ELH's verbatim WHEN–THEN rules are self-authored, so a lineage could become more persistent and also less faithful to anything it started with. Divergence should be measured against the lineage's earlier snapshot, not only against its latest state.

Weight-based personas are the contrast case. Narrow fine-tuning can switch on a broad "misaligned persona", controlled by identifiable SAE features and reversed by a few hundred benign samples ([Wang et al., ICLR 2026](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2506.19823)). Unintended personality shifts from fine-tuning track movement along persona vectors, which can be read from activations before the model responds ([Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.21509); [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/persona-vectors)). Weights give persistence and breadth at the cost of targeting. Frozen weights plus memory give reversibility and auditability: a snapshot can be diffed, swapped or deleted. ELH's frozen-weights premise rules out a fine-tuned arm. Persona-vector projection on the local Qwen3-Coder is, however, an optional way to check whether memory shifts internal state or only surface behaviour. That direction has not been validated on Qwen3-Coder, and steering a mixture-of-experts model may behave differently (unverified).

A persona that survives may also cost performance. Personas in system prompts do not improve factual accuracy across 162 roles ([Zheng et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10054v2)). Sociodemographic personas degrade reasoning, with drops above 70% on some datasets ([Gupta et al., ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04892)). No study tests work-style personas on coding success, so pass rate must be reported next to every persona metric.

## What remains untested is exactly what ELH can test

Across the three note sets, the same gaps recur, and each is an ELH contribution if done properly. First, **no study replicates the same experience history** several times to separate history-driven divergence from sampling noise, in any agent domain, let alone coding. Second, **no study measures a memory-induced trait shift within one frozen model** with psychometric criteria (ICC, MTMM, invariance). The trace-trait literature compares models, not memory lineages of one model, and Han et al.'s closest analogue found persona text did not move behaviour. Third, **no study tests causal attribution by memory transplant, ablation or placebo**. Fourth, **no study tests whether a self-acquired style survives interference or a snapshot restore** in an external-memory agent. ContextEcho's snapshot-then-probe protocol is the closest, but it measures drift from an assigned persona in long single sessions, finds compaction does not reset drift and finds a single anchor restores it ([arXiv 2605.24279](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24279)). Fifth, **no controlled study compares one persona delivered as a static prompt, as a re-injected prompt and as self-written memory** over a long task stream with opposing interference. PersMem covers a fragment of this; the rest is open.

Two of these connect to the earlier dataset report. That report argued that, with few lessons, an all-rules-in-the-prompt control should recover most of a memory gain, and that a frozen-final-memory-as-prompt control had never been run ([Memory vs prompt datasets](Memory%20vs%20prompt%20datasets.md)). The persona version of that control is the same object. Take a lineage's final bank, render it as a static block, and inject it identically every episode. If that block reproduces the lineage's style, the "personality" lives in the text and is not a property of retrieval. That is still a legitimate memory-made personality, but it should be reported as a portable artefact, not as retrieval dynamics. The `house_lib` family, which is designed around conditional, unguessable house conventions ([design doc](../docs/design/house-lib-family.md)), is also the natural second task family for cross-situational tests. A cpu_mem lineage's caution should show up on house_lib tickets whose lessons it never saw.

The strongest falsifier also deserves a plain statement. With frozen weights, everything a lineage "is" lives in its bank and its rules. A snapshot restore should therefore reproduce the style exactly, up to sampling noise, and a failure to do so indicates uncontrolled state or nondeterminism in the harness, not learning. The restore test is a validity check on the harness. The scientifically interesting retention tests are dilution (keep learning on neutral tickets) and interference (tickets that reward the opposite strategy), both without restore.

## A replicated-lineage design separates history from noise

### Controls and sizes

The unit of analysis is a **lineage**: the frozen model plus one bank and rule set grown from one training sequence. Three lineages cannot support an ICC. The notes recommend **at least 8–10 independent lineages per memory condition** so that between-lineage variance is estimable. No power guidance specific to agent personality exists, so the pilot's variance components should set the confirmatory number. Each lineage is measured on a fixed **probe battery** at each checkpoint, through read-only snapshots, which ELH already supports with `open_readonly` and per-episode `memory_snapshot_id` ([SPEC](../SPEC.md)). The battery spans at least two task families (cpu_mem causes and house_lib), plus tickets similar and dissimilar to each lineage's early training, to test the experience-following prediction. Each probe ticket runs **3–5 times per snapshot**. Within-snapshot replicate variance is the noise floor that every between-lineage claim must clear, and given 2–4 distinct paths per 10 identical runs, fewer than three replicates leaves it unestimated.

The required controls follow from the evidence. A **noise null** comes from stateless group A run with the same replicate structure, plus a Bierbach-style set of lineages trained on the *same* ticket sequence. The first measures pure sampling variance. The second separates experience content from the developmental noise of memory writing. A **placebo memory** is a bank of matched length and format with neutral or shuffled content; it controls for "any block of text in the first message". A **swap test** transplants lineage X's frozen bank into fresh runs and checks that X's profile follows the bank, and an **ablation** deletes it and checks the profile reverts to A's. These two establish causation and are the intervention counterpart to Han et al.'s null. For any seeded-persona arm, a **persona-reinjection control** puts the same persona text, as a static block, in the same first-message slot every episode (SPR in ELH's terms), alongside the frozen-final-memory-as-prompt control. That separates "memory content" from "text placed near the generation point". **Competence adjustment** means every trait model includes pass/fail and an A-labelled ticket-difficulty stratum, trait scores are compared within strata, and pass rate is reported as a second axis next to every style metric.

**Retention** is tested in three ways. A test-retest ICC after a further N neutral tickets measures dilution. An interference phase of tickets that reward the opposite style (for example, tickets where reading widely before editing times out) tests whether the style persists or fades toward recency, as PATH-Bench predicts. A snapshot restore after interference is the harness validity check. The Freund question, whether between-lineage variance grows with tickets experienced, needs at least two checkpoints per lineage.

### Analysis

Fit, per trait indicator, `behaviour ~ condition + task_family + pass + difficulty + (1 | lineage) + (1 | lineage:snapshot) + (1 | ticket)`. Report repeatability with CIs via `rptR`. The claim "distinct between lineages" requires lineage variance clearly above the A-replicate and same-sequence nulls. Add a model-free summary, **fingerprint attribution**: a classifier assigns held-out episodes to their lineage under leave-ticket-out splits. Its accuracy is compared with the same classifier's accuracy on A's replicate "lineages", which should sit at chance. Build the MTMM matrix with a trace-count and an LLM-judge rating per trait, test configural and metric invariance across conditions, and collect an end-of-episode self-description as a separate dependent variable to test for the self-report–behaviour gap. Logging persona-vector projections on the local model is optional and exploratory.

### The cheap first step, and why it cannot settle anything

Experiment 13 ran groups A, B and C on the same 15 training and 12 test tickets in three histories. Each history shuffled the training order, and each (history, group) started from an empty bank. Test passes were **A 2·3·1, B 11·12·3, C 11·10·2**. In histories 1 and 2, **31 of 60 stored rules** mentioned the ticket's second slow lookup; in history 3, **0 of 30** did, and **45 of history 3's 47 timeouts** replaced `seen` with a set and left the second list unchanged ([study report](../docs/study-report.html)). That is clear path dependence, and it is exactly the knowledge-type difference that must not be called personality.

The reanalysis extracts the trace indicators above from the existing JSONL traces for every test and training episode. It then does five things. It fits the mixed model with history as a random effect within B and within C, residualised on pass and ticket. It uses **group A's three "histories" as a free noise null**: A carries nothing, so any between-history variance in A is pure sampling and infrastructure noise. It runs leave-ticket-out fingerprint attribution of B episodes to history, against the same attribution on A. Because B and C in one history share a training order but keep separate banks, it asks whether B-h1 sits closer to C-h1 than to B-h2, which is a weak Bierbach-style check on sequence content. Finally, it uses the logged retrieved-memory IDs to see whether the recalled rules predict the action-level indicators, a mediation signal and not a causal one.

Its limits are severe and should be stated up front. Three lineages per group give no usable ICC. Each ticket ran once per snapshot, so within-lineage noise is confounded with ticket. Evaluation used one cause (`quadratic_membership`) and one theme, so there is no cross-situational test. There was one checkpoint, so there is no retention or growth curve. There was no placebo, swap or reinjection control. Group A also lacks a memory block, so it is a noise null for history but not a placebo for prompt length. History 3's competence collapse will dominate any raw style difference, and with only 3 of 12 passes the competence-stratified comparisons have almost no data. The step is useful as a kill switch and an instrument shakedown. If competence-adjusted B indicators show no between-history variance above A's, or fingerprinting cannot beat A's chance-level baseline, the personality framing should give way to the plain lineage-knowledge framing before any GPU time is spent. If a signal appears, it justifies steps 1–2 and nothing more.

## Conclusion

The literature supports a reframing rather than a yes or no. "Does the agent have a personality?" is unanswerable with questionnaires and trivially yes at the level of model identity. The open, answerable question is whether **variance between memory lineages of one frozen model is repeatable, competence-independent, portable across task families and caused by the bank**. ELH is unusually well placed to answer it. Its models are frozen, its banks are snapshot-able and swappable, and its traces are already logged with recall IDs, so the causal tests that psychology can only approximate (transplant one individual's history into another) are cheap here.

The likeliest outcome, given experience-following, recency-dominated retrieval and the Assistant Axis pull of coding work, is that lineages differ mainly in knowledge and only weakly in disposition, with styles that fade under interference. That would still be a publishable negative: it would bound how much "individuality" an external memory can carry. The result that would change the field's understanding is the opposite one. A bank that, transplanted into a fresh run on an unrelated task family, reproduces a distinct working style that a placebo and a re-injected hand-written persona prompt do not, would show that memory carries a portable disposition, not just facts. Whether the frozen-bank-as-prompt control also reproduces it then decides if that disposition lives in the bank's text alone or needs retrieval. Experiment 13 cannot show that. It can show, within a day, whether it is worth looking.
